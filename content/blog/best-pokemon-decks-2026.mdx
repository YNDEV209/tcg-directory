## The 2026 Standard Format at a Glance

The 2026 Standard rotation shook things up in a big way. With sets prior to Paldea Evolved rotating out, players lost access to staple cards that had defined the meta for over a year. What emerged is a format that rewards adaptability — aggressive decks, setup-heavy strategies, and energy acceleration engines all have a place at the top tables. Whether you're preparing for a Regional Championship or your local league challenge, knowing the top decks and how they operate is essential.

Here are five of the strongest decks in the current 2026 Standard metagame.

## Charizard ex

[Charizard ex](/cards/sv3-125) has been the deck to beat since its release in Obsidian Flames, and it continues to dominate in 2026 Standard. The strategy is straightforward: set up Charizard ex as fast as possible and start swinging for massive damage.

### Strategy

The deck uses [Rare Candy](/cards/sv1-191) to evolve directly from [Charmander](/cards/sv3-27) into Charizard ex, skipping the Stage 1 entirely. Charizard ex's Burning Darkness attack scales damage based on the number of Prize cards your opponent has taken, meaning it hits harder as the game progresses. Paired with [Arven](/cards/sv1-186) for consistent Trainer search, the deck sets up reliably by turn two.

### Key Cards

- [Charizard ex](/cards/sv3-125) — your main attacker with escalating damage output
- [Pidgeot ex](/cards/sv3-164) — provides deck search every turn through its Quick Search ability
- [Rare Candy](/cards/sv1-191) — enables the fast Stage 2 evolution line
- [Arven](/cards/sv1-186) — grabs key Items and Tools on demand

### Strengths and Weaknesses

Charizard ex's biggest strength is its consistency. Between Pidgeot ex and Arven, you rarely miss the cards you need. The damage ceiling is also enormous in the late game. However, the deck struggles against Water-type attackers and can stumble if Charmander gets knocked out early before you evolve. Decks that spread damage to the bench can also disrupt the Pidgeot ex engine.

## Dragapult ex

[Dragapult ex](/cards/sv5-130) brings a unique combination of spread damage and raw power that makes it one of the most versatile decks in the format.

### Strategy

Dragapult ex's Phantom Dive attack deals 200 damage to the active Pokemon and places 6 damage counters across your opponent's bench however you choose. This constant chip damage adds up quickly, softening targets for future knockouts. The deck often pairs with [Xatu](/cards/sv4-71) to move damage counters around for precise math, turning near-misses into clean KOs.

### Key Cards

- [Dragapult ex](/cards/sv5-130) — spread attacker with strong base damage
- [Drakloak](/cards/sv5-129) — solid Stage 1 with a useful ability to find Dreepy from your deck
- [Xatu](/cards/sv4-71) — repositions damage counters for calculated knockouts
- [Super Rod](/cards/sv2-188) — recovers Pokemon and Energy from the discard pile

### Strengths and Weaknesses

The spread damage plan is incredibly effective against decks that rely on low-HP bench sitters like Pidgeot ex. Dragapult ex can pick apart support Pokemon while pressuring the active position simultaneously. On the downside, the deck needs a few turns to get rolling and can fall behind against hyper-aggressive strategies that take early Prize cards.

## Gardevoir ex

[Gardevoir ex](/cards/sv1-86) is the format's premier energy acceleration deck, capable of powering up massive attacks out of nowhere.

### Strategy

The engine revolves around Gardevoir ex's Psychic Embrace ability, which attaches Psychic Energy from the discard pile to your Pokemon — at the cost of placing damage counters on them. Combined with attackers like [Scream Tail](/cards/sv4-86) and the mirror-match threat of a second Gardevoir ex, the deck can respond to almost any board state with the right energy attachments.

### Key Cards

- [Gardevoir ex](/cards/sv1-86) — the core energy engine
- [Kirlia](/cards/sv1-84) — its Refinement ability draws cards while fueling the discard pile
- [Scream Tail](/cards/sv4-86) — a flexible single-prize attacker
- [Cresselia](/cards/sv6-74) — recovers energy and heals damage from Psychic Embrace

### Strengths and Weaknesses

Gardevoir ex excels in longer games where it can build an overwhelming energy advantage. The ability to power up any Psychic Pokemon instantly gives it incredible flexibility. The downside is that Psychic Embrace damages your own Pokemon, making them vulnerable to Boss's Orders plays. The deck also requires careful resource management — burning through Energy too fast can leave you stranded.

## Lugia VSTAR

[Lugia VSTAR](/cards/sv4pt5-132) is a powerhouse that cheats out powerful Colorless Pokemon with its Summoning Star VSTAR Power, creating explosive mid-game turns.

### Strategy

The deck uses Lugia VSTAR's ability to place [Archeops](/cards/sv4pt5-134) directly onto the bench from the discard pile. Archeops's Primal Turbo ability then accelerates Special Energy from the deck, enabling attacks that would otherwise take several turns to power up. Once the engine is online, you can stream heavy hitters every turn.

### Key Cards

- [Lugia VSTAR](/cards/sv4pt5-132) — the deck's centerpiece with its game-changing VSTAR Power
- [Archeops](/cards/sv4pt5-134) — provides repeatable Special Energy acceleration
- [Gift Energy](/cards/sv5-171) — draws cards when the attached Pokemon is knocked out
- [Capturing Aroma](/cards/sv2-153) — helps find key Pokemon early

### Strengths and Weaknesses

When Lugia VSTAR sets up, it's one of the most explosive decks in the format. The energy acceleration is nearly unmatched, and the variety of attackers you can include gives it strong matchup coverage. The weakness is that the deck lives and dies by hitting its VSTAR Power at the right time. If Archeops is prized or if you can't get them into the discard pile, the whole strategy falls apart.

## Roaring Moon ex

[Roaring Moon ex](/cards/sv4-124) is the format's premier aggressive deck, designed to take knockouts from the very first turn of the game.

### Strategy

Roaring Moon ex's Frenzied Gouging attack can deal up to 220 damage on your first turn going second, at the cost of discarding two Energy. The deck pairs this with [Dark Patch](/cards/sv4-172) and other energy recovery to keep the pressure relentless. It aims to take all six Prize cards before the opponent's strategy comes online.

### Key Cards

- [Roaring Moon ex](/cards/sv4-124) — the hyper-aggressive main attacker
- [Dark Patch](/cards/sv4-172) — accelerates Dark Energy from the discard pile
- [Professor Sada's Vitality](/cards/sv4-170) — recovers Ancient Pokemon and attaches energy
- [Bombirdier](/cards/sv4-156) — searches out Supporter cards to keep the aggression flowing

### Strengths and Weaknesses

Speed is everything with this deck. If you can take two or three early knockouts, most opponents never recover. It punishes slow setups ruthlessly. The trade-off is that Roaring Moon ex gives up two Prize cards when knocked out, and the deck runs out of steam if the game goes long. Against bulky decks that can survive the initial onslaught, the lack of a backup plan becomes a real problem.

## Advice for New Competitive Players

If you're just getting into competitive Pokemon TCG, picking a deck is only the first step. Here are a few things to keep in mind:

**Start with one deck and learn it deeply.** It's tempting to switch between archetypes, but understanding every line of play with a single deck will win you more games than hopping between whatever topped the last Regional.

**Practice your matchups.** Knowing how your deck plays against the other top decks in this list is more valuable than any tech card you could add. Test games against Charizard ex and Dragapult ex especially — you'll face them constantly.

**Watch your resources.** The best players track what's in their discard pile, what's been prized, and what's left in their deck. This habit takes time to build but separates good players from great ones.

**Don't neglect your Supporter line.** Cards like [Boss's Orders](/cards/sv1-172) and [Iono](/cards/sv2-185) can swing games on their own. Make sure you're running enough disruption alongside your draw Supporters.

The 2026 Standard format rewards both preparation and adaptability. Pick a deck that fits your playstyle, put in the reps, and the results will follow.
